Indicators Used to Deduce a "Pocket Pivot"A Pocket Pivot (originally developed by internal portfolio managers of William O'Neil + Co.) is uniquely distinct from a standard chart pivot because it identifies accumulation inside a base rather than waiting for a classic breakout.Traders deduce this state by analyzing two primary variables concurrently:Price Actions (Moving Averages): The stock must be in an established uptrend, structurally trading above its 10-day and 50-day moving averages. The specific "pocket pivot" buy day happens when the stock bounces or breaks out from one of these key moving averages.Volume Anomalies: The day's trading volume must be higher than the highest down-day volume seen over the preceding 10 trading days. This tells the trader that buying conviction is outstripping recent selling pressure.2. Can it be used in an automated system?Yes. This signal is highly rule-based and easily converted into algorithmic or automated system code. To configure an automated scanner (e.g., Python, ThinkOrSwim, or TradingView), the code structure enforces the following script conditions:textCondition 1: Close > MovingAverage(10) AND Close > MovingAverage(50)
Condition 2: Close > Open (Up day)
Condition 3: Close crosses above or bounces off MovingAverage(10) OR MovingAverage(50)
Condition 4: Volume > Max(Volume Where Close < Open over last 10 days)
Use code with caution.If an algorithmic scanner detects all 4 variables simultaneously flashing True, the stock enters "Active Pocket Pivot" status automatically.3. Success and Correctness RateThe historical success rate of a standalone pocket pivot typically ranges between 50% to 60%.While this sounds low, it operates effectively because of an asymmetrical risk-to-reward ratio:When right: The stock enters a rapid institutional markup phase, capturing large double-digit upside moves.When wrong: It "fails" immediately by breaking back below the support moving average. This gives traders an instant stop-loss marker (typically 1–2% below the moving average bounce), resulting in small, highly controlled losses.4. Better Indicators for "Institutional Accumulation"No indicator is 100% correct due to changing market regimes, fakeouts, and algorithm-driven market makers. To achieve a higher confidence signal, professional traders combine pocket pivots with secondary structural indicators:Accumulation/Distribution (A/D) Rating: Look for stocks with an A or B rating, indicating institutional buying over the last 13 weeks.Chaikin Money Flow (CMF): Measures volume-weighted accumulation over a 21-day period; a CMF value above +0.20 confirms heavy institutional buying.On-Balance Volume (OBV) Breakouts: If OBV is making new highs even though the actual stock price is still sideways in a base, institutions are quietly scooping up shares.Volume Preceding Price: 3 or 4 consecutive up-weeks on above-average volume, matched with down-weeks on dry, micro-volume.
